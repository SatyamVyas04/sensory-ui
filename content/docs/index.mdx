![sensory-ui — shadcn/ui, with sounds](/readme-banner.png)

Interfaces ship silent. Every button, dialog, and toggle in your app already communicates through motion and color — but never through sound. The web's best component libraries have zero audio story, and the tools that do exist treat sound as decoration: generic effect players with no meaning, no system, and no respect for the user.

sensory-ui is the opposite bet: **sound as semantics**. Every cue maps to an interaction type across 17 roles and 5 categories. Every pack plays the same language in a different voice. Everything is opt-in, accessibility-first, and synthesized live with the Web Audio API — zero audio files.

## Why this exists

I kept noticing the gap: visual design systems obsess over spacing scales, motion curves, and color tokens — then leave the entire auditory channel untouched. Products either stay mute or bolt on random click sounds that annoy faster than they inform. There was no shadcn-native way to add *meaningful* audio feedback: typed roles, swappable packs, per-category kill-switches, and `prefers-reduced-motion` baked in from the start. So I built it.
